## Fixed endpoints and one-sided traces

The differentiated integral uses the same \(X_-,X_+\) at every \(\eta\). The Lean lemma proves the interior derivative using a constant majorant from compactness of the joint continuity rectangle. At \(\eta_0=\pm1\), the same conclusion for the inward difference quotient follows when \(f\) and the inward derivative \(d\) are jointly continuous on the closed inward rectangle and \(d\) is the ordinary interior derivative. The one-variable mean-value theorem bounds the absolute remainder
\[
 \left|\frac{f(\eta,X)-f(\eta_0,X)}{\eta-\eta_0}-d(\eta_0,X)\right|
 \le \sup_{e\text{ between }\eta_0,\eta}|d(e,X)-d(\eta_0,X)|.
\]
Uniform continuity gives convergence to zero uniformly for \(X\in[X_-,X_+]\). The integral of the remainder is bounded by \(X_+-X_-\) times this same uniform bound. Iterating the argument requires joint continuity of each new derivative. The needed original native regularity at arbitrary order remains an explicit premise, as does the source's separate leading mass identity when \(n=1\).
